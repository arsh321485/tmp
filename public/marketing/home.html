The challenge

Plans that sit on a shelf fail on the day.

Attacks move faster than any team can respond from memory. Most incident and continuity plans are out of date, untested, and unclear about who does what — until it’s too late to find out.

Why test regularly

Eight reasons to test your business continuity plan regularly.

The threat landscape changes every week. Your incident response and continuity plans should be proven against it just as often.

01Cyber threat landscape changes

New threats and attack vectors emerge constantly. Testing shows whether you are ready for the latest ones.

02Detection and response capabilities

Assess how well you detect and respond to malware outbreaks, data breaches and ransomware before they hit operations, data integrity and reputation.

03Assessing incident response plans

Simulated attacks and breach scenarios reveal weaknesses such as delayed detection, ineffective containment or inadequate communication.

04Backup and recovery validation

Confirm backups are complete, accessible and intact, and that recovery restores critical systems promptly.

05Evaluation of cybersecurity controls

See how your preventive, detective and response controls perform in real-world scenarios — and where the gaps are.

06Training and awareness

Most incidents start with human error. Drills teach best practice and make sure everyone knows their role.

07Compliance and regulatory requirements

Demonstrate to regulators that you safeguard data, maintain operations and can recover from cyber incidents.

08Stakeholder confidence and reputation

Regular testing reassures customers, investors and your board that cyber risk is managed — protecting trust.
